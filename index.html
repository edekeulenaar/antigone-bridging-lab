Week 4 · Bridging

This bridging section is part of the methodological cookbook series on the practical and empirical questions of how to identify and study so-called speech grey zones.

Data: the Datasets ↗. Each step below has a page on this site.

Goal

In week 2, fact-checks were used as entry points into speech grey zones. In week 3, these were described: how fact-checkers and community notes disagreed, what commenters said about notes, and what they agreed and disagreed on.

This week's assignment moves from describing the conflict to intervening in it.

The intervention is called bridging: a mechanism for facilitating understanding — the possibility that users who make conflicting statements have the information they need to understand and engage with each other. Bridging is a framework, not a single technique. Following this week's reading, each operationalisation names three things:

partquestion it answers
bridging goalWhat would count as mutual understanding?
relation metricWhat measurable property of a comment stands in for that goal?
attention allocatorWhat does the platform do with it? Here: how does it order the comments?

The goal of the assignment is to take one disputed X thread, define three bridging metrics, code every comment of the thread by them, and reorder the thread so that comments with bridging properties come first.

Six steps

  1. Pick a post

    Choose one of the disputed posts (posts where the fact-checker's verdict and the community notes reach opposite conclusions) and press Work on this post. Download its thread as a CSV.

  2. Close read the thread

    Open each statement, read the comments that make it, and discuss the questions on the page. For each answer, note two or three comments as evidence.

  3. Define your bridging metrics

    Define three metrics: properties of a comment that, if comments with them came first, would help other sides understand each other. For each, write a name, what it captures, and a coding rule.

  4. Code the thread with an LLM

    The page writes the prompt from your post and your metrics / codebook. A chatbot adds metric_A, metric_B and metric_C, with yes or no for every comment.

  5. Upload the coded thread

    Upload the coded CSV. If the chatbot returned several parts, upload them all at once.

  6. Order the thread

    Decide how the thread is ordered, in up to three levels, from engagement, chronological order and your three metrics. Choosing a metric moves every comment that has it to the top. Community notes and replies are shown separately.

How your work is saved

Results are saved automatically in your browser under Your saved results. The report (Word or PDF) is the appendix to hand in.
