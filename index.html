Design a bridging mechanism for X's comment threads

In week 2 we used fact-checks as gateways into speech grey zones. In week 3 we described them: how fact-checkers and community noters disagreed, what commenters said about community notes, and what they agreed and disagreed on. This week we stop describing the conflict and try to intervene in it.

The intervention is called bridging: a mechanism for facilitating understanding, i.e. socio-informational coherence — the possibility that users with conflicting statements have the information they need to understand and engage with each other. Bridging is a framework, not a technique, so it can be operationalised in many ways. Following this week's reading, every operationalisation names three things:

partquestion it answers
bridging goalWhat would count as more mutual understanding here?
relation metricWhat measurable relation between users stands in for that goal?
attention allocatorWhat does the platform do with the metric: rank, group, summarise, label?

Your assignment: design a bridging mechanism for one disputed X thread. The bridging goal is to facilitate understanding between divided users in X comment spaces. Your metrics, and the new order of the thread they produce, are yours to invent and to justify.

Six steps

  1. Instructions

    This page: read it to the end.

  2. Posts

    Pick a disputed post: one where the fact-checker and the community notes reach opposite conclusions. Download its thread as a CSV, with the statements the Datasets already found in it, or with an empty Statement column to code yourself.

  3. Sample

    If you code the thread yourself, first draw a sample of its comments (100 to 150 is enough).

  4. Code

    Write the statement each comment makes in the Statement column, then read the thread closely: what is the majority opinion, what is the minority's, and where do users misunderstand or misrepresent one another?

  5. Expand your coding with an LLM optional

    Turn your codebook into a prompt for a chatbot, let it code the rest of the thread, and check its coding against yours.

  6. Bridge

    Define three bridging metrics, see the thread reordered by each, and put your sample in your own bridging order by hand.

Working in groups

Work in groups of three or four, one post per group (tell me if two groups want the same post). This site keeps everything you do in your own browser: nobody else sees your uploads, codes, metrics or orderings, and nothing you do changes the site for anyone else. To share work within your group, download your files (each page has a download button) and send them to each other.

What to hand in

#whatfrom
1The case. Your post, and why it is a speech grey zone: which actors classify it, in which competing ways, and what is at stake. Two or three paragraphs.Posts
2The statements and your close reading. Your codebook; the majority and the minority opinions; where users misunderstand, misrepresent or ignore one another.Code
3Optional: how you used an LLM and how far its coding agreed with yours.Expand with an LLM
4Three bridging metrics, each defined clearly enough that someone else could apply it, with what it puts at the top of the thread and what it would miss; and your own bridging order of the sample.Bridge
