Expand your coding with an LLM optional

Let a chatbot code the rows you have not coded, with your codebook — then check, on the rows you coded yourself, how often it agrees with you. A coding you have not checked is not a finding.

3

Run it in your chatbot

  1. Open the chatbot you use — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Mistral, DeepSeek or another — in a new conversation.
  2. Attach your CSV (the sample, or the whole thread) with the paperclip or + button, and paste the prompt in the same message. Send it.
  3. Get the file back. Download the CSV it returns. If it prints the CSV as text instead, copy all of it into a plain-text editor and save it with the extension .csv.
  4. Check that nothing went missing: the returned file must have as many rows as yours, with a new LLM_Statement column. Free versions often stop part-way through long files: if rows are missing, split your CSV into parts of about 100 rows and send each part with the same prompt, in a new conversation each time.
  5. Note the model's name and the date. Use the same model for every part.
4

Verify the results

The model writes its codes in LLM_Statement and leaves your Statement column as it was, so the two sit side by side.

  1. Take the rows you coded by hand (20 to 30 at least) and compare Statement with LLM_Statement, row by row. In a spreadsheet, a new column with =IF(TRIM(A2)=TRIM(B2), 1, 0) (with your two columns in place of A and B) marks the rows where they agree; its average is the share of rows on which you agree.
  2. Look statement by statement. For each statement, count how often the model gave it where you did not (it over-assigns), and how often it missed it where you gave it (it under-assigns). One overall share hides which statements the model cannot see.
  3. Read every disagreement. Look for patterns: irony and sarcasm, replies that only make sense against the comment they answer, translations, statements whose definitions overlap.
  4. Improve and run again. If you agree on fewer than about seven rows in ten, sharpen the definitions in your codebook (what counts, what does not), make the prompt again, and re-run it.
  5. Report it: the model, the share of agreement, the statements it gets wrong, and what you changed.

When you are satisfied, open the file in Bridge and choose LLM_Statement as the statement column. Your codebook is remembered in this browser, so Bridge can show your statements' wording.
